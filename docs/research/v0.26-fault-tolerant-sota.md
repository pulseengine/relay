# v0.26 — Fault-Tolerant Control (Rotor Loss) SOTA (research note)

Scope: rotor/motor-failure tolerance for the falcon quad, on the existing
stack (IEKF estimator, geometric SE(3) `relay-geo`, ADRC `relay-adrc`,
Kani-verified mixer `relay-mix-quad`). Mandate: write to the intersection
of what is *mechanically provable*.

## 1. Quadrotor single-rotor-failure — the canonical result

A quad cannot hold full attitude on three rotors (too few independent
torques). **Mueller & D'Andrea's relaxed-hover family** resolves it: relax
"hover" from a fixed attitude to a **periodic solution constant in the body
frame** — the equilibrium angular velocity is zero or **parallel to
gravity**. The failsafe **spins the airframe continuously about a primary
body axis (near-vertical)** and **relinquishes yaw**. Retained: **total
thrust** (height) + **tilt of the spin axis** (S² reduced attitude → the
time-averaged horizontal acceleration → position). Flight-validated for 1
and 2 (opposing) rotors. [Berkeley HiPeRLab PDF](http://hiperlab.berkeley.edu/wp-content/uploads/2018/05/2014_StabilityAndControlOfAQuadrocopterDespiteTheCompleteLossOfOneTwoOrThreePropellers.pdf),
[IJRR 2015](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1177/0278364915596233).

The clean modern framing is **reduced-attitude control on S²** (stabilise
the thrust-axis direction almost-globally, abandon the S¹ heading)
[arXiv:1906.06926](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1906.06926),
[arXiv:1704.00327](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1704.00327) — maps directly onto
`relay-geo`: drop the yaw channel, keep the SO(3)/S² thrust-direction law.

**Hexa/octo do NOT trivially keep full control** — a hexa is typically
**yaw-uncontrollable after one rotor loss**; same fix (ignore yaw,
reallocate thrust), controllable iff per-rotor max lift clears a threshold.
[arXiv:1307.0276](https://arxiv.org/abs/1307.0276),
[MDPI Actuators](https://www.mdpi.com/2076-0825/10/3/48).

## 2. Fault detection & isolation (FDI)

- **Estimator-innovation / residual-based**: a rotor loss spikes the
  observer innovation — directly reusable with the project's **IEKF
  innovations + NEES** machinery.
- **Effectiveness-factor estimation** (strong baseline): a KF estimates a
  per-actuator effectiveness factor from **RPM + gyro + accel**, then a
  hypothesis test isolates the failed rotor — **30–130 ms latency, zero
  false alarms / misses** over 26 real ejections
  [arXiv:2102.06439](https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.06439).
- **Adaptive-threshold / CUSUM on a whitened residual** suppresses false
  alarms even for merely *damaged* props.

Recipe: **CUSUM / adaptive-threshold test on a whitened residual**
(effectiveness-factor or IEKF innovation), isolate by which rotor's
effectiveness collapses — the structure NEES already provides.

## 3. Reconfiguration / allocation on failure

Standard active-FTC: **zero the failed column of the effectiveness/B
matrix** and re-solve over healthy rotors
[arXiv:2011.00481](https://arxiv.org/abs/2011.00481). Pseudo-inverse is
simplest but ignores saturation; **redistributed pseudo-inverse /
active-set** respects rotor limits. For the **quad**, reallocation alone is
insufficient (rank deficiency) → **switch controller mode** to the
reduced-attitude/spin law with **tilt-priority desaturation**.

Composes with `relay-mix-quad`: failure = **swap to a reduced B (drop a
column), drop the yaw row**, keep the floor/priority/airmode policy now
read as *tilt-priority* desaturation. Hexa/octo stay in normal allocation
with a reduced actuator set.

## 4. What is formally verifiable (ranked)

1. **Kani bound on the reconfigured allocator output set** (direct
   extension of MIX-P05/06/07): for *every* single-column-dropped B and all
   bounded demands, allocated commands stay in `[floor, max]` and the
   failed rotor stays at 0. **Highest-value, lowest-risk** — precedent:
   octorotor envelope verified in dReal/SMT that the pseudo-inverse
   allocator never exceeds rotor capability under stuck-rotor faults
   [arXiv:2107.00612](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2107.00612).
2. **FDI soundness bound**: Kani/Lean argument that the threshold test
   cannot fire below a residual floor (no false alarm) and must fire above
   another (detection guarantee) — a latency/false-alarm contract.
3. **Lyapunov / invariance for the reduced system** (Lean/Rocq): almost-
   global stability of the S² thrust-axis law + forward-invariance of a
   hover tube — heavier; leans on the v0.23 geometric-control proof.

## 5. Newer (2024–2026)

- **INDI fault-tolerant control** (Sun & Wang) is SOTA: sensor-based,
  stabilises **double-opposing-rotor loss at >8 m/s** in wind tunnel,
  beats LQR [arXiv:2002.07837](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2002.07837). Pairs
  naturally with the ADRC inner loop (both disturbance-rejecting,
  incremental) but is **harder to verify** — keep as an unverified ceiling.
- **Learning-based FDI** (LSTM/RNN ~99% isolation) — not provable;
  out-of-scope for the certified core.
- **Verdict**: Mueller–D'Andrea reduced-attitude **remains the right
  provable baseline** for quad single-rotor loss.

## 6. Recommendation for v0.26 (ranked, achievable)

1. **FDI (must-have)**: per-rotor **effectiveness-factor estimator from
   IEKF innovations + RPM**, **CUSUM/adaptive-threshold** isolation reusing
   NEES/YawObservability. Target ≤130 ms, zero false alarms.
2. **Reconfiguration**: on isolated single-rotor loss, **drop the failed
   column + yaw row of `relay-mix-quad`'s B**, switch `relay-geo` to
   **reduced-attitude (S²) mode** (yaw relinquished), keep ADRC, apply
   **tilt-priority desaturation**.
3. **The gate (defines v0.26)**: a **Kani oracle — MIX-P08/RECONFIG —
   proving the reconfigured allocator's output set stays in `[floor,max]`
   with the failed rotor pinned to 0, for all single-column-dropped B and
   all bounded demands**, plus an **FDI false-alarm/detection-threshold
   bound**. Defer the full Lyapunov-on-S² proof to a follow-on; ship the
   bounded allocator + FDI contracts first as the certified floor.
