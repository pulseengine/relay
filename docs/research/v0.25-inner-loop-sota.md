# Background research — v0.25 robust inner loop SOTA (May 2026)

Scouted while building v0.23/v0.24. Now *empirically the critical path*:
the v0.24 investigation proved the residual yaw spin is an
**actuator-dynamics instability in the yaw control loop** (not
estimation, not allocation, not disturbance — YAW_OFF is perfectly
stable, tight-compass still spins, open-loop has zero yaw disturbance).
The fix is a robust inner loop that accounts for actuator dynamics.

## Recommendation: ADRC (geometric extended-state-observer) — best;
## INDI — runner-up / proven fallback; L1 adaptive — verification-exists option

**ADRC / geometric ESO on SE(3)** is the best fit for a *formally-
verified* quad:
- It lumps all unmodeled torque (incl. actuator-lag-induced yaw, rotor
  drag) into a **"total disturbance" extended state**, estimates it with
  an observer, and cancels it.
- Its core guarantee is an **ESO observer-error Lyapunov / finite-time
  bound** — an *algebraic* Lyapunov argument, far closer to our SMT/SOS/
  Kani tooling than INDI's two-time-scale singular-perturbation proof.
  *Geometric ADRC of Rotorcraft on SE(3) with Fast Finite-Time
  Stability* (Wang, Hamrah, Sanyal, Glauser, arXiv:2306.08090, 2023)
  proves disturbance-estimation convergence AND tracking **together on
  SE(3)** — so it composes with our v0.23 geometric outer loop in one Lie-
  group Lyapunov framework (no time-scale-separation patch).
- Targeted at exactly our problem: the residual is the lumped
  disturbance it estimates.

**INDI** (runner-up): most flight-proven (PX4, agile research), rejects
the disturbance well — but its published stability result (Wang/van
Kampen/Chu/Lu, JGCD 2019, DOI 10.2514/1.G003791) is **local, gain/
bandwidth-conditioned, via singular perturbation** — the *least*
machine-checkable shape (two-time-scale Tikhonov argument). Keep as the
proven fallback + v0.19→v0.24 continuity.

**L1 adaptive** (close third, co-leader under a reachability-verification
weighting): the ONLY candidate with an existing **machine-checked
(reachability) verification on quadrotors** (*Verification of L1 Adaptive
Control using Verse*, arXiv:2303.13819, 2024) and explicitly built to
**augment geometric SE(3) control with proven transient tubes** (*L1Quad*,
arXiv:2302.07208). If our verification leans on Kani-style bounded model
checking rather than theorem proving, L1 may be the fastest path to a
machine-checked inner loop.

## Design guidance (all three)

- Feed the inner loop **IEKF-bias-corrected RAW gyro** through its own
  synchronized filter — NOT the fully-fused IEKF rate (delay mismatch).
  Take the bias estimate from the IEKF; do the differentiation/filtering
  in the inner loop.
- Angular-acceleration estimate (INDI/ADRC need ω̇): 2nd-order filter on
  the gyro, **delay-synchronized** with the actuator-state estimate
  (motor commands through a 1st/2nd-order lag = the motor time constant
  that caused our yaw instability). Synchronization is what the stability
  hinges on.

## Honest provability tradeoff

No option has a *global, already-machine-checked* inner-loop proof today.
INDI/INDB: best heritage, weakest mechanization (singular perturbation).
ADRC: best theorem *shape* for mechanization (algebraic/finite-time
Lyapunov, SE(3)-native) — but we'd be writing the Lean/Coq proof. L1:
only one with an existing reachability artifact.

**v0.25 path:** ADRC/ESO primary inner loop; IEKF-bias-corrected raw gyro
+ synchronized 2nd-order filter; INDI fallback; target the ESO observer-
error Lyapunov bound as the first machine-checked theorem (L1's
reachability approach as backstop).
