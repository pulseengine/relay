# Position-hold A/B investigation — synthesis (the breakthrough)

Date: 2026-05-30. Two parallel subagents resolved the yaw/position-hold
problem that an entire session of hand-tuning could not.

## Method

- **Track B** (exclusive Gazebo): systematic one-parameter-at-a-time
  re-tune of the √-corrected stack via the JSON config, requiring 3/3+
  reproducible PASS (final_dist < 0.5 AND rms_steady < 1.0).
- **Track A** (code + web, no gz): structural comparison of our stack
  against PX4's proven multicopter controller.

They DIVERGED in diagnosis — which is what made running both decisive.

## Track B — what empirically works (it found a reliable config)

Track B achieved **6/6 PASS** (reproduced 4/5 here, ~0.3 m typical — a
categorical change from the session's 6–500 m). Parameters, by impact:

1. **Yaw torque is effectively INVERTED in the geo-hover path.** The
   dominant bug. `--scenario=frame-yaw` was *too noisy to detect it*
   (it reported AGREE in one run, OPPOSE in another). Decisive empirical
   test: same config, `adrc[2].b0 = −6` → **3/4 PASS**; `b0 = +6` →
   **1/4 PASS**. Negative b0 flips the ADRC control AND its plant model
   consistently, cancelling the inverted actuator. (Durable fix: a yaw-
   sign correction in the Rust path — root cause still TBD, frame-check
   oracle unreliable.)
2. **`hover_thrust = 0.555`** (from 0.52): the √-PWM linearization moved
   the true hover point; the altitude loop has no integrator so it must
   match gravity (0.52 sank to ~0.9 m, >0.60 climbed). Altitude offset
   couples into the horizontal limit cycle, so nailing it was essential.
3. **`pos_var = 0.005`** (from 0.04): the horizontal loop damps on the
   IEKF velocity; loose pos_var lagged it → a ~6 s-period circular limit
   cycle (1–3 m). Tightening cut the lag and the orbit damped out.
4. **Gentle horizontal gains** `kp_pos=0.08, kd_vel=0.6, a_cmd_max=1.0`
   (from 0.6/1.2/3.0): high gains excited the limit cycle; a small
   nonzero kp centres the drone without exciting it.
5. **`mixer_floor=0.2`** (from 0.5): yaw headroom in the priority mixer.

This re-tune is now the `falcon-config` default + `configs/falcon-quad.json`.

## Track A — what makes it ROBUST (the structural path)

Track A found three PX4 safeguards we lack, against the weakest/laggiest
axis — these are not gain-fixable and explain the residual ~1/5 fragility:

1. **Rate loop at 100 Hz; PX4 runs ~1 kHz** (every gyro sample). The yaw
   actuator pole is 25 ms (1/τ≈40 rad/s); a 100 Hz loop (+ZOH delay) is
   already near phase margin. (Our SDF IMU is even 200 Hz.)
2. **No gyro low-pass filter** before the ESO. PX4 has
   `IMU_GYRO_CUTOFF=30 Hz`. Our high-gain ESO (β2=ω_o²=900 on yaw)
   differentiates raw, noisy gyro → the disturbance estimate chases noise
   into the motors. The crate's own "k_R kept low or it spins" comment IS
   this symptom, worked around by detuning.
3. **Hard 0.5 floor vs airmode.** When a yaw correction pushes a motor
   below the floor, `scale_to_fit` scales yaw to zero — authority vanishes
   nonlinearly exactly when needed (limit-cycle recipe). PX4 airmode
   *boosts collective* instead. (Mitigated, not solved, by floor 0.2.)

## Synthesis — they're complementary, not contradictory

- **Track B got it FLYING** (the √-fix had invalidated the gains; B
  recovered them + found the yaw sign) → mostly-passing, ~0.3 m.
- **Track A is the path to RELIABLE** (1 kHz loop + gyro LPF + airmode) →
  would eliminate the residual 1/5 near-misses and run-to-run variance.

Neither alone was the answer. The A/B comparison cut through an
investigation that blind tuning could not.
