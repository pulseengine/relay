# Falcon — v1.0-Practical Readiness Dossier

*Capstone of the v1.8 → v1.15 hardware-abstraction arc. Consolidates what is
proven, what is simulated/emulated, and — honestly — what still needs real
hardware before this flies an aircraft.*

Date: 2026-06-03 · Scope: the `falcon-core` verified cascade + its backends,
drivers, proofs, and embedded target.

---

## 1. What Falcon is, in one paragraph

A **backend-agnostic, formally-anchored multirotor flight core**. The verified
cascade — Invariant-EKF estimator → geometric SE(3) attitude → ADRC inner loop
→ control allocator — runs unchanged behind a hardware-abstraction seam
(`FlightBackend`). The *same* `no_std` code flies three backends: an in-process
simulator, real sensors via driver traits, and a hardware-in-the-loop link to a
remote simulator or vehicle. Autonomy (a guarded flight-mode FSM), failsafes
(geofence→RTL, low-battery), robustness (injected sensor pathologies), timing
(a kernel-checked compositional WCET argument), and qualification posture (a
single math seam for libm) are all in place — **in simulation, proof, and
emulation**. It has **not** flown on hardware; the seam is where that plugs in.

## 3. The honest gap register — what needs YOUR board

These are **not** done and are **not** faked anywhere in the tree. They are the
work between "v1.0-practical in SITL/emulation" and "flies an aircraft":

1. **Real sensor/actuator driver bodies + on-silicon validation.** The seam and
   one reference IMU driver (`falcon-imu-icm42688`) exist; the register protocol
   and scaling are mock-bus tested. A real `embedded-hal` SPI/UART/PWM bus, CS
   timing, FIFO/data-ready, bus-fault handling, and validation on the actual
   chips remain. The GNSS/mag/ESC/battery drivers are seam-only.
2. **Sensor calibration** — bias/scale/axis & board-mounting remap on the real
   units (the IMU driver uses an identity remap placeholder).
3. **Flight tuning** — the control gains are SITL-tuned; the real airframe needs
   on-vehicle tuning.
4. **WCET leaf measurement** — the compositional schedulability proof is
   complete and sound; its per-stage leaf *budgets* are conservative
   declarations, not yet measured Cortex-M7 cycle counts.
5. **libm qualification** — the seam makes it a one-crate change; the actual
   qualification (a proven core or measured conformance to tolerance) is unmade.
6. **Physical HITL transport** — the link protocol + closed loop are verified
   over an in-process loopback; a real UART/USB/UDP transport and its
   latency/jitter/reconnect handling remain.
7. **First flight** — by definition, requires the above and a vehicle. No claim
   of flight is made anywhere.

## 6. Verdict

For a **research / SITL / emulation** v1.0, Falcon is complete and the claims
hold under independent check. For a **hardware-flying** v1.0, the seven items in
§3 stand between here and first flight — every one of them on the *hardware*
side of the abstraction seam this arc was built to make swappable. The honest
answer to "what would we really need": items §3.1–§3.7, on your board. Nothing
in the tree pretends otherwise.
