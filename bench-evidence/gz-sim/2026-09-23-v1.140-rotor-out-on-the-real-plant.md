# Rotor-out recovery does NOT hold on the gz plant (v1.140, #398)

**Date:** 2026-09-23 · **Commit:** `e1d00be` (main) · **Rig:** gz sim 8.15.0,
`worlds/falcon-quad.sdf`, macOS arm64 · **Cited by:** FV-FALCON-FAULT-005

## What was being established

FV-FALCON-FAULT-005 is the gz half of SWREQ-FALCON-FAULT-P02 — the last
engineering item in v1.140's scope. The mock half was tightened in #474 so the
verdict stops accepting a falling vehicle. This run asks the question that
matters: **does the same judgement pass on the real plant?**

It does not. **Twelve pristine-server trials: 11 FAIL, 1 PASS** (the first four are tabled below; the full tally and the root cause follow).

## The control — the rig is healthy

Run first, on the same server generation, so a rotor-out failure cannot be
confused with a bench that cannot fly:

A 2 m hold to 0.11 m with 1 cm of horizontal drift. The plant, the bridge and
the tuning are all working. Whatever follows is about the rotor loss.

The single PASS matters for how this is read: the property is **intermittent,
not uniformly broken**. A gate sampling it a few times can report success —
which is exactly the hazard named below, now demonstrated rather than argued.
Any future campaign for this property needs enough trials to see a 1-in-12
outcome, and must key on the gate state at the kill rather than averaging tilt.

## ROOT CAUSE (added after the first four trials — `FDI_TRACE`)

**The gz hover roll/pitch limit cycle sits astride the FDI detection gate, so
whether a rotor loss is detectable at all is decided by the phase of that limit
cycle at the instant of failure.**

The detector only runs while `fdi_steady` holds:

```rust
let rp_rate2 = gyro_f[0]*gyro_f[0] + gyro_f[1]*gyro_f[1];
let fdi_steady = tilt_cos > 0.90 && rp_rate2 < 4.0;  // ≲26°, ≲2 rad/s roll+pitch
```

Traced over the 25 ticks **before the rotor is killed** — a healthy, passing
hover — `rp_rate2` oscillates **3.596 … 4.521**, straddling the 4.0 threshold.
The vehicle hovers at 1.90–2.12 rad/s roll/pitch against a 2.0 rad/s gate.

**The gate is shut 15 of those 25 ticks — 60%.** (This came from `FDI_TRACE`, i.e. the signal the gate actually reads. A later "correction" to 96.8% was computed from the RAW gyro and is WITHDRAWN; re-measured properly it is 33.8% pre-kill.)

The code's own comment says the gate was raised from 1 to 2 rad/s because "the
REAL gz plant hovers with an attitude limit-cycle near 1 rad/s roll/pitch, which
held this gate chronically shut". The limit cycle is now ~2 rad/s. The margin
that raise bought has been consumed.

### The prediction, and the test

If this is the mechanism, the gate's state **on the kill tick** should determine
the outcome. Traced, one row per trial:

| trial | gate at kill tick | isolated | peak tilt after |
|---|---|---|---|
| G2 | **OPEN** | Some(0) — within 1 tick (+0.004 s) | 1.012 rad |
| G3 | **shut** | **None** | **3.142 rad (180°)** |
| G4 | **OPEN** | Some(0) | 1.126 rad |
| G5 | **OPEN** | Some(0) | 1.458 rad |
| G6 | **OPEN** | Some(0) | 1.139 rad |

**5 of 5.** Gate open on the kill tick ⇒ isolation, every time. Gate shut ⇒ never
isolated, and the airframe inverts.

It is one-way: once the rotor is gone the vehicle departs, `tilt_cos` falls and
`rp_rate2` stays high, so a gate that was shut at the kill **never reopens**.
G2's trace shows the gate closing at +0.024 s and staying shut while `tilt_cos`
walks 0.99 → 0.68 over the next 0.4 s. A failure that lands in a shut phase is
undetectable for the rest of the flight.

### Two defects, not one

1. **Detection is a coin flip.** Cause above. It is *not* a defect in the FDI
   residual logic — when the gate is open the residual is unambiguous
   (`resid=[1.000 0.000 0.000 0.000]`) and isolation happens in a single tick.
2. **Even with immediate isolation, the recovery is weak.** G2/G4/G5/G6 all
   isolated within a tick and still peaked at **1.01–1.46 rad (58–84°)** against
   FAULT-003's analytic-plant claim of 0.274 rad worst-case. Perfect detection
   would not by itself make this property hold.

### The fix is not the gate

Raising the threshold again would make the runs pass without making the vehicle
safer — the same vacuous-gate shape this release train has been finding all
along. **The limit cycle is the defect** — and it is NOT a hover: independent
reproduction measured the vehicle still in `mode=Takeoff` at the kill (1.49 m
against a 2.0 m target, climbing at 0.09 m/s). It is a saturated climb that
never reaches hover. ~2.5 rad/s RMS
roll/pitch oscillation during a hover the position loop reports as good
(0.11 m). The position hold masks an attitude loop that is ringing. That is
worth checking against the wobble the bench engineer reported from the
Wireshark capture, which had the same character.

## What the numbers say

**Two distinct failure modes, from the same binary on the same world.**

1. **Total inversion (rot1).** The FDI never isolated the dead rotor
   (`isolated=None`) and the airframe reached 3.142 rad — exactly inverted. It
   never landed and never disarmed; it was still tumbling when the run ended.
   This is the `no tumble` clause of FAULT-P02 failing outright.

2. **Isolate-and-come-down (rot2/rot3/diag).** The FDI *did* isolate rotor 0,
   the motor failsafe commanded LAND, and the vehicle touched down and disarmed
   within ~1 s of the loss. But peak tilt after the loss is **0.87–1.27 rad
   (50–73°)**, against a 0.5 rad bar, and it travels **3.9–8.4 m** getting there.

**The run-to-run spread is the finding, not noise to average away.** Whether the
FDI isolates at all flips between runs of an unchanged binary against an
unchanged world. 1 of 4 clean trials never isolated. An intermittent FDI on a
safety property is worse than a consistently failing one, because a campaign
that samples it a few times can report success.

Note the near-miss: rot1's 9.55 m horizontal is **inside** the 10.0 m threshold.
Had it also isolated, that trial would have failed only on tilt. The horizontal
bar is not carrying much weight here and should not be read as validated.

## What this does NOT say

- **Nothing about the wasm cascade.** `--scenario=supervised-rotorout` drives
  the **native** `FlightSupervisor`. `main.rs` states plainly that *"the wasm
  cascade flies this same FlightCore with NONE of this tuning"* — and the gz
  branch of this scenario applies `set_pos_var` / `set_process_floor` /
  `set_altitude_gains` / `set_altitude_integral_gain` that the component does
  not receive. #398 was reported against the **wasm** cascade, so this
  measurement is adjacent to that report, not a reproduction of it. The wasm
  leg lives in `tests/cascade-sitl-wasm` (gazebo.yml), not in this binary.
- **Nothing about the cause.** No instrumented trace was taken. The FDI
  intermittency and the 50–73° excursion are *observations*; attributing them
  (allocator rank-deficiency behaviour, detection latency under real rotor
  dynamics, the tuning gap above) needs the `fdi_diag()` trace that
  FV-FALCON-FAULT-004 used, and that work is not done here.

## This falsifies an artifact that is already `verified`

FAULT-005 staying `proposed` understates the result. `SWREQ-FALCON-FAULT-P02`
carries six `verified-by` links and **five of them are `verified`**. One of
those is directly contradicted by the table above.

**FV-FALCON-FAULT-003** (v1.114, `verified`) ends with:

> FALSIFICATION: this recovery is wrong if a single-rotor-out from a settled
> hover is observed to exceed ~45° tilt, spin past ~10 rad/s, or fail to reach
> a near-upright landing — **on the analytic plant (this oracle) or the gz
> plant**.

Every one of the four trials exceeds 45°: 49.7°, 50.8°, 72.9°, 180°. The
artifact named the gz plant itself, so this is its own stated kill-criterion
being met — not an out-of-scope plant being held against it.

It is sharper than that. FAULT-003's description says the defect it fixed was a
flip "to ~180° (**reproduced on the real gz plant** and, now, offline)". A 180°
flip on the gz plant is exactly what rot1 shows. Either that fix did not hold on
gz, or something since has regressed it.

**What is NOT claimed:** that FAULT-003's own oracle is wrong. Its analytic-plant
numbers (worst peak tilt 0.274 rad over 200 dispersed full-loop trials) are a
different measurement and were not re-run here. The gap is between that oracle
and the gz plant — the same campaign-vs-real-plant gap that
`relay-rotor-out-parasitic-and-idealized-harness` already cost once, and that
FAULT-003 was itself written to close.

`FV-FALCON-SIMMC-005` (1500 dispersed trials, worst peak tilt 1.20 rad against a
1.4 rad tumble bound) is also `verified` on a non-gz plant and worth re-reading
in that light, though its bound is looser and this measurement does not directly
breach it.

## Consequence for the release

**FV-FALCON-FAULT-005 stays `proposed`.** The evidence required for `verified`
does not exist: the property does not hold on the plant it claims to hold on.
v1.140 therefore cannot reach 11/11 by measurement — only by a scope decision.

**And FV-FALCON-FAULT-003's status is now a maintainer decision**, because a
`verified` artifact whose falsification condition has been met is the one thing
the release process is not allowed to leave alone. Demoting it is a scope call
that needs explicit confirmation; so is deciding the gz clause was over-reach in
the falsification text. Either way it should not stay silently `verified`.

## Falsification

This finding is wrong if `--scenario=supervised-rotorout` on `--backend=gazebo`
produces `isolated=Some(0)` with `peak_tilt_after < 0.5` on three consecutive
pristine-server trials at this commit. It was not observed once in four.
